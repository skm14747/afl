## What a synastry chart is

A synastry chart takes two individual birth charts and compares them. Every planet in Person A's chart is measured against every planet in Person B's chart. The angular relationships between these cross-chart planet pairs are called aspects, and they form the core of synastry analysis.

Unlike a composite chart, which merges two charts into one, synastry preserves each person's chart intact. You can see exactly how your Mars interacts with your partner's Moon, or where your Saturn lands in their house system. Synastry is the most granular technique in relationship astrology.

## Aspects: the angular relationships

An aspect is the angle between two planets measured along the zodiac. Five major aspects are used in synastry:

**Conjunction (0°)** — Two planets occupy the same degree or very close to it. The energies merge. A conjunction between charts means that area of life is a point of intense focus for both people. Whether the conjunction is harmonious or overwhelming depends on the planets involved. Venus conjunct Jupiter feels generous and expansive. Mars conjunct Pluto feels powerful and potentially volatile.

**Sextile (60°)** — A supportive angle connecting compatible elements. Sextiles indicate areas where two people naturally cooperate. The energy is helpful but mild — sextiles facilitate connection without forcing it.

**Square (90°)** — A tense angle connecting clashing elements within the same modality. Squares create friction, but friction generates energy. In synastry, squares between personal planets often indicate the areas where a couple pushes each other to grow. The tension is real, but so is the engagement.

**Trine (120°)** — A flowing angle connecting the same element. Trines feel easy. Two people with strong trine contacts understand each other with minimal effort in those areas. The risk of trines is complacency — the connection is so smooth that neither person is challenged to develop.

**Opposition (180°)** — A polarizing angle connecting opposite signs. Oppositions create awareness of what each person lacks. They can feel magnetic or adversarial. In synastry, oppositions often describe the dynamic where each partner provides what the other needs but cannot generate alone.

### Minor aspects

Beyond the major five, some astrologers also consider minor aspects in synastry:

- **Quincunx (150°)** — an awkward angle between signs that share neither element nor modality. Quincunxes require constant adjustment. In synastry, they can indicate areas where two people repeatedly misunderstand each other despite good intentions.
- **Semi-sextile (30°)** — a mild, slightly uncomfortable angle between adjacent signs. The connection exists but requires translation.

## Orbs: how close is close enough

An orb is the degree of leeway allowed when determining whether an aspect is active. If a conjunction is exact at 0°, an orb of 8° means any planet pair within 0–8° of the conjunction angle is counted.

Orb allowances vary by tradition, but a common synastry framework is:

- **Conjunction and opposition**: 8–10° orb
- **Trine and square**: 6–8° orb
- **Sextile**: 4–6° orb
- **Minor aspects**: 1–3° orb

Tighter orbs produce stronger effects. A Venus-Mars conjunction at 1° apart is felt far more acutely than the same conjunction at 9°. When reading a synastry chart, prioritize close aspects. A tight square between personal planets will likely be more noticeable in daily life than a wide trine.

The Sun and Moon are typically granted the widest orbs because they represent the most fundamental parts of personality. Outer planet aspects (Uranus, Neptune, Pluto) are often read with tighter orbs because their effects are generational and less personally felt unless contacting a personal planet closely.

## House overlays: where planets land

House overlays answer the question: where do your partner's planets fall in your chart? This is separate from aspects. Even if no aspect exists between two planets, the house placement of your partner's planets in your chart colors how you experience their energy.

### How house overlays work

Each person's birth chart has twelve houses, determined by their birth time and location. When you overlay your partner's chart onto yours, their planets fall into specific houses of your chart. The house they land in describes the life area where you feel their influence most.

**1st house overlay** — You are very aware of this person. Their planet in your 1st house affects how you see yourself. A partner's Sun in your 1st house makes you feel seen. Their Saturn in your 1st house makes you feel scrutinized.

**4th house overlay** — This person affects your sense of home and emotional foundation. Planets here create a domestic connection. A partner's Moon in your 4th house often indicates someone who feels like home.

**5th house overlay** — Romance, pleasure, and creativity. Planets here spark joy and play. Venus or Mars in your 5th house from a partner's chart is a classic indicator of romantic attraction.

**7th house overlay** — The house of partnerships. When someone's planets land here, you instinctively view them as a partner. Sun, Moon, or Venus in the 7th house overlay is one of the strongest indicators of relationship potential.

**8th house overlay** — Intimacy, vulnerability, and shared resources. Planets here create deep but sometimes uncomfortable connections. The bond feels transformative. Mars or Pluto here intensifies the sexual dimension.

**12th house overlay** — The house of the unconscious, isolation, and transcendence. Planets here create a connection that is hard to articulate. The relationship may feel spiritual, confusing, or both. There can be a sense of past-life recognition.

## Reading the full grid

A synastry chart generates a grid showing every cross-chart aspect. Reading this grid requires prioritization. Not every aspect carries equal weight.

**Start with personal planets.** Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus, and Mars contacts between charts describe the day-to-day experience of the relationship. These are felt directly and consciously.

**Then check Saturn.** Saturn contacts describe the commitment dimension — where the relationship is tested and what holds it together long-term.

**Then check the outer planets to personal planets.** Uranus, Neptune, or Pluto contacting a personal planet can indicate powerful but destabilizing dynamics. Uranus contacts bring excitement and unpredictability. Neptune contacts bring idealization and potential confusion. Pluto contacts bring intensity and transformation.

**Finally, check the Ascendant and Midheaven.** Planets contacting these angles describe how the relationship is perceived — by the partners themselves and by the outside world.

## What synastry does not show

Synastry maps the potential dynamics between two people. It does not map effort, communication skills, emotional maturity, or life circumstances. Two charts can show strong compatibility and the relationship can still fail if the people are not ready for it.

Synastry also does not capture timing. A chart comparison looks the same whether the two people meet at 20 or at 50. Transit analysis — tracking the current positions of planets against both charts — adds the time dimension. What synastry shows as potential, transits reveal as activated.

## A practical approach

When reading a synastry chart, resist the urge to tally up "good" and "bad" aspects. A relationship with all trines and no squares may be comfortable but unstimulating. A relationship with strong squares and oppositions may be challenging but deeply engaging.

Look for balance. The best synastry charts, in terms of relationship sustainability, usually contain a mix: enough flowing aspects for ease, enough tense aspects for growth, and at least one strong Saturn contact for endurance.
