## What Guna Milan is

Guna Milan — also called Ashtakoot Milan or Ashtakoota matching — is the Vedic astrological system for assessing marriage compatibility. It compares the birth Moon's nakshatra (lunar mansion) and rashi (zodiac sign) of two people across eight categories, each called a koot or koota.

The system assigns points to each koot, totaling a maximum of 36. The combined score indicates the degree of compatibility between two prospective partners. This method has been used in Hindu marriage traditions for centuries and remains the most widely consulted compatibility framework in Vedic astrology.

## The eight koots

### 1. Varna (1 point)

Varna assesses spiritual and ego compatibility. It classifies each person into one of four varnas based on their Moon sign: Brahmin (priestly), Kshatriya (warrior), Vaishya (merchant), or Shudra (service). The classification has nothing to do with caste in social terms — it describes the temperament of the Moon sign.

One point is awarded when the groom's varna is equal to or higher than the bride's in the traditional hierarchy. The logic is that the partner with the broader spiritual outlook can accommodate the other's perspective. In modern practice, many astrologers apply this bidirectionally.

### 2. Vashya (2 points)

Vashya measures mutual influence and control in the relationship. Each Moon sign is classified into one of five categories: Manav (human), Vanchar (wild), Chatushpad (four-legged), Jalchar (aquatic), or Keeta (insect).

The scoring checks whether the two categories have a natural dominance or compatibility. Full points go to pairs with mutual influence. The underlying principle is that neither partner should completely dominate the other.

### 3. Tara (3 points)

Tara evaluates health and well-being compatibility by comparing the birth nakshatras. The 27 nakshatras are counted from one person's birth nakshatra to the other's, and the result is divided by 9. The remainder determines the tara category.

Certain remainders are auspicious (indicating mutual well-being), and others are inauspicious. If both partners' taras are mutually favorable, full points are awarded. Tara is considered important because it relates to the physical and psychological health of the couple together.

### 4. Yoni (4 points)

Yoni assesses sexual and physical compatibility. Each nakshatra is assigned an animal symbol — horse, elephant, deer, snake, dog, cat, rat, cow, buffalo, tiger, hare, monkey, lion, or mongoose. Each animal has a natural friend, enemy, or neutral counterpart.

If both partners share the same yoni animal, full points are awarded. Enemy animal pairings score zero. This koot carries the most weight among the first four because physical compatibility is considered foundational for marriage longevity.

### 5. Graha Maitri (5 points)

Graha Maitri evaluates mental and intellectual compatibility by comparing the lords of the two Moon signs. Each Moon sign has a planetary ruler, and the relationship between those two rulers — friend, neutral, or enemy — determines the score.

If both rulers are mutual friends, full points. If one is a friend and the other is neutral, partial points. If the rulers are enemies, zero points. This koot addresses whether two people think in compatible ways and can sustain conversation and intellectual respect over decades.

### 6. Gana (6 points)

Gana assesses temperament and social behavior. Each nakshatra belongs to one of three ganas: Deva (divine, mild-mannered), Manushya (human, balanced), or Rakshasa (fierce, intense).

Same-gana pairings score full points. Deva-Manushya pairings score partial points. Deva-Rakshasa pairings typically score zero because the temperamental gulf is considered too wide. In practice, Rakshasa gana often gets an unfair reputation — it describes intensity and directness, not malice.

### 7. Bhakoot (7 points)

Bhakoot checks the relationship between the two Moon signs directly. It evaluates the angular distance between them — essentially the same concept as aspect analysis in Western astrology but applied specifically to the Moon signs.

Certain combinations are considered doshas (flaws). The 2/12 combination is associated with financial difficulty. The 6/8 combination is associated with health problems and conflict. The 5/9 combination is considered highly favorable. Bhakoot carries the highest points because it reflects the overall trajectory of the marriage.

### 8. Nadi (8 points)

Nadi evaluates genetic and health compatibility and carries the maximum weight. Each nakshatra belongs to one of three nadis: Aadi (beginning/Vata), Madhya (middle/Pitta), or Antya (end/Kapha).

If both partners share the same nadi, zero points are awarded — this is called Nadi Dosha and is considered the most serious incompatibility in Ashtakoot Milan. The logic is rooted in Ayurvedic principles: same-nadi couples are believed to face health challenges, particularly regarding children.

Nadi Dosha has specific cancellation conditions. If the Moon signs are the same but nakshatras are different, or if the nakshatras are the same but the padas (quarters) differ, the dosha may be nullified.

## How to read the total score

The 36-point scale has conventional interpretation ranges:

- **Below 18**: The match is considered incompatible. Most traditional astrologers advise against proceeding.
- **18 to 24**: Acceptable match. The couple will face challenges but has sufficient compatibility to build a functional marriage.
- **24 to 32**: Good match. The couple has strong alignment across most dimensions.
- **Above 32**: Excellent match. High compatibility across nearly all koots.

A score of 18 is the minimum threshold in traditional practice. However, the distribution of points matters as much as the total. A score of 20 with zero in Nadi and zero in Bhakoot may be more problematic than a score of 18 with points distributed across all eight koots.

## What Guna Milan does not cover

The Ashtakoot system is a screening tool. It evaluates Moon-based compatibility and does not examine:

- **Planetary aspects in the full birth chart**: Mangal Dosha (Mars affliction), Saturn's position, and the 7th house lord are assessed separately.
- **Dasha compatibility**: The planetary periods both partners are running affect the timing and quality of marriage. A high Guna Milan score with incompatible dashas can still produce a difficult marriage.
- **Navamsa chart**: The D9 divisional chart specifically addresses marriage and is examined alongside the birth chart in a thorough Vedic compatibility analysis.

Guna Milan gives you a structured compatibility score. For a complete marriage assessment, it should be one input among several, not the sole deciding factor.
