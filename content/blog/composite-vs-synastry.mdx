## Two techniques, two questions

Relationship astrology uses two primary chart comparison methods: synastry and composite charts. They are frequently confused, and many people assume they provide the same information in different formats. They do not.

**Synastry** asks: how do these two individuals affect each other?

**The composite chart** asks: what is the nature of the relationship itself?

The distinction matters because a couple can have excellent synastry — strong mutual attraction, emotional understanding, supportive contacts — and still produce a composite chart that describes a difficult relationship. The reverse is also possible. Understanding both techniques gives a complete picture of what a partnership holds.

## How synastry works

Synastry places two birth charts side by side and measures the aspects between every planet pair. Person A's Moon at 15° Cancer forming a trine to Person B's Venus at 17° Scorpio is a synastry aspect. It describes something specific: Person A's emotional nature flows easily with Person B's love nature. The aspect belongs to the interaction between two separate people.

Synastry preserves individuality. You can see clearly what Person A brings and what Person B brings. You can identify asymmetries — cases where one person is more affected by the other. If Person A's Saturn squares Person B's Moon but Person B's Saturn makes no hard aspects to Person A's chart, the burden of Saturn falls more heavily on Person B.

### What synastry reveals

- **Attraction patterns**: Venus-Mars contacts, Ascendant contacts, 5th and 8th house overlays
- **Emotional compatibility**: Moon-Moon aspects, Moon-Venus aspects, 4th house overlays
- **Communication dynamics**: Mercury-Mercury aspects, Mercury to other personal planets
- **Commitment indicators**: Saturn contacts to personal planets, 7th house overlays
- **Tension points**: Hard aspects between Mars and personal planets, Pluto contacts
- **Power dynamics**: Which person's planets are more heavily aspected by the other's Saturn or Pluto

Synastry is detailed and specific. It tells you exactly which parts of each person are activated by the other. The drawback is complexity — a full synastry grid contains dozens of aspects, and reading it requires careful prioritization.

## How the composite chart works

The composite chart is a single chart calculated from the midpoints of corresponding planets in both birth charts. Person A's Sun at 10° Aries and Person B's Sun at 20° Gemini produce a composite Sun at 15° Taurus (the midpoint). This calculation is performed for every planet, the Ascendant, and the Midheaven.

The resulting chart does not belong to either person. It belongs to the relationship. The composite chart describes the entity that two people create when they come together — the character of the partnership itself, its purpose, its challenges, and its public face.

### What the composite chart reveals

- **The relationship's identity**: The composite Sun sign and house describe the core purpose and focus of the partnership. A composite Sun in the 10th house suggests a relationship oriented toward shared public goals or career. A composite Sun in the 4th house centers the relationship around home and family.
- **Emotional climate**: The composite Moon describes how the relationship feels on a daily basis. A composite Moon in Gemini creates a partnership that processes emotion through conversation. A composite Moon in Scorpio creates emotional intensity as the relationship's default state.
- **How the relationship communicates**: Composite Mercury shows how the couple talks to each other and to the outside world.
- **How the relationship expresses love**: Composite Venus describes the aesthetic and romantic quality of the partnership — what it values, what it enjoys, how affection flows.
- **How the relationship handles conflict and desire**: Composite Mars shows where the relationship's energy is directed and how disagreements unfold.
- **The relationship's challenges**: Composite Saturn reveals where the partnership faces structural pressure — the area of life where the most work is required.

## Key differences in practice

### Individual vs collective

Synastry keeps both individuals visible. You can trace any dynamic back to a specific planet in a specific person's chart. The composite chart erases individual contributions. It shows the third entity — the relationship — without attributing qualities to either partner.

This matters practically. If a couple is struggling with communication, synastry can tell you whose Mercury is under pressure and from which planet. The composite chart can tell you that the relationship itself has a Mercury-Saturn square — that the partnership, regardless of who is "causing" it, tends toward serious or restricted communication.

### Attraction vs identity

Synastry is stronger at mapping attraction. The cross-chart Venus-Mars aspects, house overlays, and Ascendant contacts that describe physical and romantic chemistry are synastry territory. These are interactions between two separate energy systems, and they require two separate charts to track.

The composite chart is stronger at describing what the relationship becomes over time. Early in a relationship, synastry is more noticeable — the thrill of contact, the electricity between two charts. As the relationship matures, the composite chart becomes more relevant. The initial chemistry settles, and the relationship develops its own personality — one that may differ significantly from what either person expected based on the early dynamic.

### Timing and transits

Both charts can be used with transits (the current positions of planets in the sky), but they respond to transits differently.

Transits to a synastry chart activate specific interactions between the two people. If transiting Saturn squares Person A's Venus, their experience of love within the relationship shifts, but Person B may not feel the same transit directly.

Transits to the composite chart affect the relationship as a unit. If transiting Saturn conjuncts the composite Moon, the emotional climate of the partnership goes through a Saturnian period — heavier, more serious, more demanding — regardless of what either individual's chart is experiencing.

## When to use which technique

**Use synastry when you want to understand:**
- Initial attraction and chemistry between two specific people
- Why a particular dynamic exists and who contributes what
- Asymmetries in the relationship — where one person feels more than the other
- Specific areas of compatibility and conflict between the two charts

**Use the composite chart when you want to understand:**
- The relationship's overall purpose and direction
- How the partnership functions as an entity — its strengths and vulnerabilities
- What outsiders see when they observe the couple
- How the relationship will respond to major life transits

## Using both together

The most complete relationship analysis uses both techniques. Synastry maps the wiring between two people — the specific contacts that generate attraction, understanding, and tension. The composite chart maps the destination — what all that wiring creates when it is connected.

A couple might have synastry contacts that produce intense sexual chemistry (strong Venus-Mars aspects) and a composite chart with the Sun in the 6th house — a relationship oriented toward service, health, or daily routine rather than romance. The chemistry exists between the people, but the relationship itself is practical in nature. Neither reading is wrong. Both are true simultaneously.

This is why experienced astrologers do not rely on a single technique. The relationship between two people is not one thing. It is the interaction (synastry) and the entity (composite) together.

## Limitations of both techniques

Neither synastry nor the composite chart accounts for individual growth, life circumstances, or the choices people make. A powerful composite chart does not keep two people together if one of them decides to leave. A difficult synastry grid does not prevent a relationship from thriving if both people commit to working through the challenges.

Chart analysis reveals structure. It maps the terrain. Walking the terrain is a human activity that no chart can predict or guarantee.
